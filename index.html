PCB test automation / Hardware + software

Flying probes 🎯

During my co-op, I noticed a manual-testing bottleneck and proactively proposed an automation jig. I presented my manager with a plan covering cost, timeline, and estimated savings, and he approved it.

The Jig

  • 2 probes, with 6 I²C-controlled stepper motors - X/Y/Z per probe.
  • Coordinated through a master - slave architecture.
  • Reliable access to pins at 0.4 mm pitch.

Test App

Delivered a centralized app that organizes:
  • Interacting with Altium to get the schematics and nodes
  • Users build tests by pairing nodes and setting expected voltage and resistance.
  • The host app coordinates the flying probe, power supply, and multimeter.
  • Taking and logging measurements
  • Alerting the user if needed
  • Uploading test results to a database for data analysis (e.g., the most common defects)

Magnetic interference testbed

3-Axis Gimbal Magnetic Interference Rig

  • The problem: Magnetorquer interference can affect magnetometer readings used for satellite attitude determination.
  • What I built: A three-axis gimbal with two magnetometers, ESP32 wireless streaming, and a switchable solenoid, making it easy to experiment with various physical rejection methods and disturbance profiles in real time

The approach

Reproduce the disturbance. Test the response.
  • Use a switchable solenoid to introduce a magnetic disturbance while rotating the gimbal.
  • Stream IMU data over Wi-Fi to observe how motion and interference affect the readings.
  • Develop and compare candidate noise-rejection methods on a laptop, with the goal of protecting attitude estimates from magnetorquer interference.
